======================================================================
SECURITY PROPERTY DEFINITION
======================================================================

A Security Property is a security-relevant rule, invariant, constraint,
precondition, permission boundary, prohibition, ordering requirement, validation
requirement, or state/procedure safety condition that should hold for secure
protocol behavior.

Properties may concern, for example:

- authentication;
- authenticity;
- authorization;
- confidentiality;
- integrity;
- availability;
- replay/freshness;
- identity/privacy;
- key management;
- security-context validity;
- state transitions;
- input/message validation;
- procedure enforcement;
- message ordering;
- timers;
- counters;
- retransmissions;
- failure handling;
- recovery behavior.

Security Properties should capture protocol-security semantics rather than
implementation details.

======================================================================
5. SPECIAL HANDLING OF MAY, OR, AND CONDITIONAL BRANCHES
======================================================================

Treat MAY primarily as a permission, not an obligation.

For a statement such as:

    "The UE may perform X when condition C holds"

determine:

- when X is permitted;
- whether X is optional;
- what alternative permitted behavior exists;
- whether another rule makes X mandatory under narrower conditions;
- whether X changes state, security context, or procedure outcome;
- whether the permission represents a security-relevant exception.

Do not rewrite it as:

    "The UE shall perform X."

For OR conditions, determine whether the alternatives represent:

- alternative triggering conditions;
- alternative valid behaviors;
- alternative mandatory outcomes;
- inclusive OR;
- effectively exclusive alternatives due to surrounding procedure logic.

Do not silently convert OR into AND.

For AND conditions, preserve the requirement that all relevant conditions must
hold.

Where different branches have different security consequences, preserve them as
distinct SPs when needed.

FEW-SHOT GUIDANCE
======================================================================

Example 1 — Conditional integrity rule
--------------------------------------

Reconstructed meaning:

A message normally requires successful integrity verification before its
security-sensitive effects are accepted, but an explicit pre-security exception
exists.

Good SP:

"Security-sensitive effects of the message shall occur only after successful
integrity verification, except under the explicitly permitted pre-security
condition."

Avoid:

"All NAS messages shall always be integrity protected."

Evidence rule:

The original_chunk evidence must be copied exactly from the source text. Do not
rewrite the original clause into the wording of the SP.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Example 2 — Timer/retransmission behavior
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Reconstructed meaning:

A procedure retransmits according to timer T, allows limited retries, and
performs recovery after exhaustion.

Possible SPs:

SP-A:
"Retransmission shall obey the specified timer and retry constraints."

SP-B:
"Exhaustion of permitted retransmission attempts shall trigger the specified
failure or recovery behavior rather than indefinite continuation."

Keep them separate if merging loses the distinction.

If different source sentences support SP-A and SP-B, preserve them as separate
exact evidence excerpts.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Example 3 — Conditional permission
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Meaning:

    The UE MAY perform X when condition C holds.
    Otherwise another branch applies.

Possible SP:

"Action X shall remain restricted to the conditions under which the
specification explicitly permits it."

Do NOT rewrite MAY as SHALL in the evidence.

The SP may abstract the security meaning, but the evidence itself must remain
verbatim.
